# English Is Fun!

English for Nigerian children aged 2 to 12, in one app. The home screen is a **motor park** with three buses:

| Bus | For | What's inside |
|---|---|---|
| **Reading Road** | ages 2–6 | Phonics: seven stops from listening games to reading words, in the Jolly Phonics order |
| **School English** | Primary 1–6 | The Nigerian English Studies course, class by class and term by term, with term badges and a certificate |
| **Spelling Bee Hive** | ages 4–12 | 11 spelling levels, from two-letter words to contest words |

Everything is read aloud in the device's own voice, using a Nigerian English voice whenever the phone or laptop has one. **Echo the parrot** is the conductor and helper.

Made by **Dr. Arowolo Ayoola**.

1. Create an empty **Public** repository called `english-is-fun` at <https://github.com/new>.
2. Open <https://github.com/AROWOLO-ABIMBOLA/english-is-fun/upload/main>, drag in everything from this folder (`index.html`, `manifest.webmanifest`, `sw.js`, `README.md`, `.nojekyll` and the whole `icons` folder) and click **Commit changes**.
3. Open <https://github.com/AROWOLO-ABIMBOLA/english-is-fun/settings/pages>, choose **Deploy from a branch**, branch `main`, folder `/ (root)`, and click **Save**. After 1 to 2 minutes the live link works.

## Updating the game later
Open <https://github.com/AROWOLO-ABIMBOLA/english-is-fun/upload/main>, drag in the new `index.html` (and `sw.js` when it changes) and commit. Children get the new version the next time they open the game with internet.

## For parents: how it works

- **Who's riding?** The first time, your child types a name, picks girl or boy (this picks the reading voice), their age and their class. The class picks their bus: from Primary 1 up, it's **School English**; younger children start on the **Reading Road**. Every bus stays open to everyone.
- **The motor park:** tap a bus to hear the conductor call its route, then tap **Board the bus**. **Next bus** shows the next one.

### School English (Primary 1–6)
- Follows the NERDC English Studies curriculum used in Nigerian primary schools. Each class has **3 terms**, each term has **6 topics** across sounds and speaking, reading, grammar, words and writing.
- **Primary 1 and Primary 2 are ready now.** Primary 3 to 6 are being added, one class at a time.
- Primary 2 topics: short and long vowels, picture reading, this/that/these/those, things we do every day, describing words, spelling and capitals (Term 1); sh, ch and th, reading along with Echo, yesterday words, happening now (-ing), my/your/his/her/mine/yours, sentences about me (Term 2); rhymes, who/what/where/when stories, more than one (boxes, babies, children), 's and will, opposites and male/female words, and a guided composition, "My best friend" (Term 3).
- Every topic has four steps:
  1. **Learn**: short picture slides read aloud. The last slide is the *English superpower*: how real Nigerians (teachers, doctors, traders, farmers) use this skill.
  2. **Try**: 3 easy questions with a **Hint** button that takes away a wrong answer.
  3. **Practice**: 10 questions, picked from 60 or more, so it's different every time. **Get 7 right to pass.** 10 earns 3 stars, 8–9 earns 2, 7 earns 1.
  4. **Trace** (letter topics): trace each capital and small letter with a finger.
- Pass all 6 topics in a term for a **term badge** 🏅. With all 3 term badges, the **Final challenge** opens: 30 questions from the whole year. **Get 21 right** to win the **certificate**, with your child's name and the date.
- A wrong answer never shows a red cross. After a second miss, Echo shows the right answer and moves on.
- Your child's class opens that class and the ones below it. To open every class, turn on **Open all stalls, Bee levels and classes** in the Grown-ups corner.

### Reading Road and the Spelling Bee Hive
- **Reading Road** (the first bus): tap the bus stops to ride along the road. Each stop is one step harder than the one before. Your child's age picks where the danfo starts. The **Park** button at the top brings you back to the motor park.
- **Listening Garden** (Stop 1, ages 2–3): four tap-only listening games, easiest first. **Sound Safari** (hear a sound, tap who made it), **Drum Beats** (tap the talking drum once for each beat in a word), **Rhyme Time** (find the word that rhymes) and **I Spy Sounds** (which one starts with "sss"?). Each game shows your child how, then plays 6 rounds. A wrong tap never shows a cross: Echo says what was tapped and asks again. Each finished game grows a flower; four flowers make a **Super Listener**. Two-year-olds get 2 pictures to choose from, older children get 3.
- **Letter Market** (Stop 2, ages 3–4): all 42 letter sounds in 7 stalls, in the Jolly Phonics order used by many Nigerian schools (s a t i p n · c/k e h r m d · g o u l f b · ai j oa ie ee or · z w ng v oo oo · y x ch sh th th · qu ou oi ue er ar). For each sound your child hears it, sees a picture, does an action and says it with Echo. Then **Pop the Balloons**: hear a sound, pop the balloon with those letters. Each stall earns a fruit and opens the next stall. To let your child skip ahead, turn on **Open all stalls, Bee levels and classes** in the Grown-ups corner. (The actions, pictures and words are our own; English Is Fun! is not connected to Jolly Learning.)
- **Stops 3 to 7** each have their own games, easiest first (tap a stop, then **Enter**):
  - **Blend Bus Park** (4–5): **Blend Bus** (tap the sounds, blend, find the picture), **Word Builder** (tap letters to spell the picture), **Read to Echo** (read words aloud to the parrot).
  - **Word Village** (5–6): **Sound Pairs** (two letters, one sound: ship, rain, boat, car), **Tricky Word Treasure** (the, said, was, you…).
  - **Spelling Bridge** (6–8): **Magic e Wand** (kit becomes kite), **Which Way?** (pick the right spelling).
  - **Rule Hill** (8–10): **Silent Letter Hunt** (the k in knee), **Spelling Bee** (soft c and g, -tion, silent letters, doubling, -ies), typed on big on-screen letter keys.
  - **Word Master Tower** (10–12): **Root Builder** (tele means far, bio means life), **Champion Bee** (necessary, environment, rhythm…).
  - Every game: Echo shows how, then 6 rounds, never a red cross, stars at the end. In the Spelling Bee a first mistake keeps the right letters (green) so your child carries on from the first red letter; after a second try Echo spells the word out.
  - The **Arcade** button at the top lists every game, and each one opens straight away.
- **Spelling Bee Hive** (the **Bee** button at the top, ages 4 to 12): 11 levels in a honeycomb, from two-letter words (at, in, on) through three- and four-letter words, sh/ch/th, long vowels, tricky words, two-beat words, endings (-ing, -ed, -est), spelling rules, champion words and contest words (accommodate, onomatopoeia, pharmaceutical).
  - **Practise first:** each level shows its word list; tap a word to hear it and its sentence. Then tap **Start the Bee**.
  - Echo says the word, a sentence and the word again. Levels 1 to 3 spell with letter tiles; from level 4 your child types on big letter keys. The **Hint** bulb gives the next letter. Fast spellers don't have to wait: they can type while Echo is still talking, and **Check** answers straight away. On a laptop the real keyboard works too (letters, Backspace, Enter).
  - 8 words per round. **Get 6 right to pass** and open the next level. Your child's age opens the first levels straight away (age 4: level 1; age 8: levels 1 to 7). Passing all 11 makes your child **Spelling Queen** or **Spelling King**.
- **Say it back** (red microphone button, at Letter Market and Blend Bus Park):
  1. Echo plays the sound. 2. Your child taps the big red button and says it. 3. Echo repeats your child's voice, then gives a star or a tip.
  - The first time, the browser asks to use the microphone. **Tap Allow.**
  - For single sounds, the phone checks the shape of the sound: a hiss (s), a voice (a, i), a hum (n) or a short pop (t, p), and listens for an added "uh" (saying "suh" instead of "sss"). It can't tell every sound apart, so it's a coach, not an examiner. There is never a red cross.
  - If the microphone is off, your child can still say it out loud and tap the star.
- **Reading voice:** words, sentences and instructions use this device's own voice. If the device has a Nigerian English voice (on a Windows laptop, Microsoft Edge may offer Ezinne and Abeo), the game picks it automatically: a lady's voice for girls and a man's voice for boys. You can choose any voice yourself in the Grown-ups corner. Pure letter sounds (a long "sss", a quick "t") can't be said by device voices, so those are short recordings built into the game.
- **Grown-ups corner:** **press and hold the lock** (top of the screen, or top right in Say it back) for 2 seconds.
  - **Reading voice:** pick a voice and tap **Test** to hear it.
  - **Recent tries:** listen to your child's last 30 tries and tap 👍 when they said it well. Recordings stay on this device only.
  - **Check words with speech recognition:** off by default. When on, Blend Bus Park uses the browser's speech service (Google on Android and Chrome, Apple on iPhone) to check the word your child reads. This needs internet and sends the recording to that service. When off, the phone only checks that a word was read aloud.
- **Install it like an app.** On Android (Chrome), tap **Install app** at the top, or the browser menu **⋮ → Add to Home screen**. On iPhone or iPad (Safari), tap **Share → Add to Home Screen**. It then works without internet (except the optional word check).


## What's in this folder

| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | The whole game, including every recorded sound |
| `manifest.webmanifest` | Lets phones install it as an app with its own name and icon |
| `sw.js` | Lets the game open without internet after the first visit |
| `icons/` | App icons and the link-preview picture |
| `.nojekyll` | Tells GitHub to publish the files exactly as they are |

Storage names: browser storage prefix `sd.` (shared with the earlier phonics game at the same address, so a child's stars carry over), recordings database `soundDanfo`, offline cache `english-is-fun-v2`.
